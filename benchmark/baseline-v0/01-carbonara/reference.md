## Major actions / steps
1. Slice the guanciale into strips, remove the rind, and dice into small to medium pieces.
2. Grate the cheese into a bowl.
3. Add the egg yolks to the cheese and whisk until it comes together.
4. Cook the pasta as per packet instructions.
5. Add the guanciale to a cold pan over medium heat.
6. Cook for about 4 to 5 minutes until golden and the fat has rendered.
7. Add the cooked pasta straight into the pan and mix well so the oils coat the pasta.
8. Add the egg and cheese mixture and mix really quickly to avoid clumping and scrambling.
9. Add the pasta water a little bit at a time until you get the perfect consistency.
10. Serve into bowls, top with guanciale, fresh grated cheese, and cracked black pepper.

## Explicit times / temperatures
- Medium heat (for rendering the guanciale)
- Cold pan (starting condition for the guanciale)
- 4 to 5 minutes (time to cook the guanciale)

## Important visual-only information
- The rind is shown being manually sliced off the guanciale strips and set aside.
- The egg and cheese combine into a thick, dry, yellowish paste prior to being added to the pan.
- The pasta used is a long variety (visually resembles spaghetti or bucatini).
- Pasta water is poured from a separate glass measuring cup, not straight from the boiling pot.
- Tongs and a spatula are used simultaneously to quickly toss the pasta with the egg mixture.

## Notes
- The narrator explicitly calls this a "four ingredient" carbonara to start (guanciale, cheese, eggs, pasta), treating pasta water and black pepper as secondary/garnishes.
- Unlike some traditional methods where pasta water is mixed with the egg and cheese first, here the egg-cheese paste is added directly to the pasta, followed by the water.
- The cheese is never explicitly named in the audio or text (it is just called "cheese" and "fresh cheese"), though it visually looks like Pecorino Romano or Parmigiano-Reggiano.
